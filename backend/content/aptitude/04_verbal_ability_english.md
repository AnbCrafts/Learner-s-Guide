# SECTION 1: ERROR SPOTTING & SENTENCE CORRECTION

---

### Type 1.1: Subject-Verb Agreement with "Neither/Either... Nor/Or"
* **Rule:** When two subjects are joined by *either... or*, *neither... nor*, or *not only... but also*, the verb agrees with the **closest (nearer) subject**.
* **TCS Practice Question:**
  Identify the error in the sentence:  
  *"Neither the manager nor the employees (A) / was present (B) / at the annual corporate strategy meeting (C) / yesterday (D)."*
* **Solution:**
  * The subject closest to the verb *"was"* is *"employees"* (plural).
  * Therefore, the verb must be plural: **"were present"**.
  $\implies$ **Error in Part B**.

---

### Type 1.2: One of the + Plural Noun + Singular Verb
* **Rule:** *"One of the [Plural Noun]"* always takes a **singular verb** unless preceded by a relative pronoun (*who/which/that*).
* **TCS Practice Question:**
  Fill in the blank with the correct verb form:  
  *"One of the most decorated soldiers in our regiment ________ received the national gallantry medal."*  
  A) have  
  B) has  
  C) were  
  D) are  
* **Solution:**
  The true subject of the clause is **"One"** (singular), not *"soldiers"*.  
  $\implies$ Correct verb: **"has"** (Option **B**).

---

### Type 1.3: No Sooner... Than / Hardly... When (Inversion)
* **Rules:**
  * *No sooner* is always followed by **than** (never *when*).
  * *Hardly / Scarcely* is always followed by **when** (never *than*).
  * Both trigger subject-auxiliary inversion (*No sooner had he entered...*).
* **TCS Practice Question:**
  Identify the grammatically correct sentence:  
  A) No sooner had she opened the door when the alarm sounded.  
  B) No sooner did she opened the door than the alarm sounded.  
  C) No sooner had she opened the door than the alarm sounded.  
  D) Hardly had she opened the door than the alarm sounded.  
* **Solution:**
  * (A) Incorrectly pairs *No sooner* with *when*.
  * (B) Incorrectly uses *did opened* (should be *did open*).
  * (D) Incorrectly pairs *Hardly* with *than*.
  $\implies$ **Option C** is grammatically flawless.

# SECTION 2: SENTENCE COMPLETION & PREPOSITIONS

---

### Type 2.1: Fixed Prepositions Commonly Tested in TCS
* **Cheat Sheet:**
  * *Abide* **by** the rules
  * *Congratulate* **on** success (Never *for*)
  * *Prevent / Prohibit* **from** doing
  * *Accused* **of** a crime (Never *for*)
  * *Insist* **on**
  * *Prefer* X **to** Y (Never *than*)
* **TCS Practice Question:**
  *"The senior committee strictly insisted ________ following standard operating procedures."*  
  A) on  
  B) to  
  C) for  
  D) with  
* **Solution:**
  The verb *insist* takes the fixed preposition **"on"**.  
  $\implies$ **Option A**.

# SECTION 3: PARA JUMBLES (SENTENCE REARRANGEMENT)

---

### Strategy for TCS Para Jumbles:
1. **Find the Independent Sentence (Opening sentence)**: It introduces the main noun, topic, or acronym. It never begins with pronouns (*he, she, they, this, these*) or conjunctions (*therefore, however, but*).
2. **Find Mandatory Pairs**:
   * Acronym first, abbreviation later (e.g., *World Health Organization* $\to$ *WHO*).
   * Cause and Effect (*Because... Therefore*).
   * Pronoun-Noun links.

* **TCS Practice Question:**
  Arrange the following sentences in a coherent paragraph:
  * **(P)** However, prolonged screen time can lead to digital eye strain and disrupted sleep cycles.
  * **(Q)** Smartphones have undoubtedly revolutionized modern human communication.
  * **(R)** They provide instant access to global information and bridge geographical divides.
  * **(S)** Therefore, maintaining digital discipline and taking scheduled breaks is essential for wellbeing.
* **Step-by-Step Arrangement:**
  * **Q** is the only independent introductory sentence introducing *Smartphones*.
  * **R** uses the pronoun *"They"* referring back to smartphones and explains the benefits. Pair: **Q $\to$ R**.
  * **P** introduces the contrast (*"However..."*) discussing the harms. Pair: **R $\to$ P**.
  * **S** is the concluding sentence (*"Therefore..."*).
  $\implies$ Correct Sequence: **Q - R - P - S**.

# SECTION 4: READING COMPREHENSION (RC)

---

### Key Question Types:
1. **Direct Factual Question**: Answered directly by scanning keywords.
2. **Inference Question** (*"What can be inferred from the passage?"*): Requires reading between the lines—must be logically unavoidable, not an over-generalization.
3. **Tone of the Author**:
   * *Critical / Sceptical* (Pointing out flaws)
   * *Objective / Informative* (Neutral reporting of facts)
   * *Optimistic / Commendatory* (Praising or hopeful)
   * *Nostalgic* (Looking back at the past fondly)
